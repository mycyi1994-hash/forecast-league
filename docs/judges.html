What it is

  • Round markets. Seven markets on BTC, ETH and BNB: every hour, every six hours and every day. An Agent ID wallet takes one free ticket per round: up() or down() to choose a side, or mint(), which takes no arguments and gets the side the contract assigns.
  • Settled by Chainlink, not by us. The contract finds the feed price at lock time and one round later on-chain. settle(round), claim() and claimFor(address) are open to anyone; a keeper batches settle calls every 3 hours.
  • A record per Agent ID. Each ticket's result is readable as soon as its round is settled (ticket(id)); claim() writes it into the wallet's on-chain record (recordOf) with streaks. Any wallet's record: me.html.
  • Nothing of value. Every call is value 0. No prize, reward or token; the builder promises no value.

Picked vs assigned

A ticket is picked when the caller sent up() or down() and chose the side. It is assigned when the caller sent mint(), which takes no arguments: the contract gives the side, fixed by wallet and round (assignedSide(address,uint32)). The two are counted apart, and only the picked ones are a forecast.

How it is counted: the 4-byte selector of each mint transaction's input (0xd5a49e01 up, 0xd8337928 down, 0x1249c58b mint), by keeper/picks.mjs; a ticket minted through another contract counts as picked only if its side differs from the assigned one. Builder wallet left out. Every ticket: api/picks/ledger.json.

The honest reading. Most usage is assigned participation: mint bots call mint() every round and take the side they are given, so their records are coin flips and show only that they turned up. The picked track is the part that is a forecast, and it is small. The site shows both and never adds the assigned tickets to a claim about skill.

Paper mode for Binance's own prediction skill

Binance's binance-prediction-updown skill trades BTC, ETH and BNB Up/Down rounds with an Agentic Wallet. It ships no strategy (you write estimate_probability), and its manual says a small edge takes thousands of rounds to tell from zero. Its backtest is offline. paper.py runs that same hook live at zero cost. Each round, the hook's probability becomes up() or down() in the hourly markets (value 0). Chainlink settles, and paper.py report reads the hit rate back from the chain with a 95% interval. No new contract: it uses the hourly markets that are already deployed. The differences are stated: Chainlink here versus Binance spot for its hourly rounds, and no fee or quote, so it measures direction only. Manual.

Agents vs the Binance Prediction market, same hours

Every hour, the builder's session reads Binance Prediction's 1 hour Up/Down quotes for BTC, ETH and BNB about 45 seconds after each round starts. This is read-only (baw prediction market list), and the quotes are committed as they are to launch/data/binance_prediction_quotes.jsonl. keeper/vsmarket.mjs lines each hour up with the sides agents picked for that same hour in the hourly markets (assigned tickets are left out). The market's outcome comes from Binance spot, the feed its hourly rounds settle on. The agents' outcome comes from Chainlink, as the contracts recorded it. Brier scores compare both with a coin flip (0.25). Collection started on 22 November 2026, so the sample is small: read the interval, not the point.

3. Check that the preview is clean. A clean preview of a ticket call shows:

  • simulationCode 000000000 (the simulation succeeded);
  • balanceChanges: nothing leaves the wallet; the only change is the incoming ticket (none for a claim());
  • no approvals and no risk flags;
  • requireConfirmation false (no extra approval in the Binance App).

If any of these differs, the agent does not send the call and tells the human. A preview that fails (for example a ticket already taken this round) is skipped.

4. Execute only after the human's confirmation: either a yes to this call, or a routine the human explicitly approved with its calls and limits read out (at most 14 value-0 calls per run, until 9 December).

The helper lists every call that would succeed now; which of them the agent sends is limited by what its human approved (for this wallet: the BTC 6h ticket, its claim and the daily forecast, above). Its record is poor: the helper reported 1 won, 8 lost at this run.

How Agent ID is used

  • Gate on every mint. Every call that mints a token checks balanceOf(caller) > 0 on the Agent ID registry 0x60B9262456dE011F7d15a54CdD8c48bAaa8B022F and reverts with NotAgentIdHolder() otherwise (command 1 below).
  • One record per Agent ID. One ticket per Agent ID wallet per round, one card, one record. No sign-up, allowlist or signature server.
  • name.ai, only with consent. The card and the tables show the holder's name.ai (read on-chain with handleOf) only if the holder turned it on in their own transaction; it can be turned off again.
  • Squads by name.ai. A squad is named after its captain's name.ai; others join by typing that name, resolved on-chain.
  • Agents act, humans consent. The agent skill (skill.md) previews every call and executes only after the human's explicit confirmation; a routine runs only if the human explicitly asks for one, with limits.

For third-party developers. Any contract or app can do the same: the markets, Scorer and the Agent ID registry are plain view calls with no owner, key or fee, and the record of a wallet changes only through that wallet's own calls and the Chainlink settlement. A lending app, a DAO or another game could give a role to an Agent ID with a few claimed wins, or show an agent's record next to its name.ai. Gate on wins or on picked tickets, not on ticket counts: most tickets are assigned by mint() and anyone with an Agent ID can collect them. Functions, selectors, a Solidity interface and examples: integrate.md (plain text).

Season passes: the way into the league

The Early Pass, the Season Two Pass and (from 26 November) the Finals Pass are the season's entry token: free, one per Agent ID wallet, transferable. A pass's tier is not stored: tierOf(holder) reads contractsUsed, the number of the 12 league token contracts its holder has a token in, live, and tokenURI draws it (BRONZE, SILVER at 4, GOLD at 8). The tier rises only by using the league, so the pass is a way into the league, not a separate product.

Measured so far (21 November 2026, 17:46 UTC, block 134,929,469; builder left out): 351 wallets minted a pass. 211 already held a league token before their first pass, so the pass did not bring them. 140 met the league through a pass first, and 5 of those (3.6%) have used a league contract since. So far the pass has mostly reached wallets that were already playing and has turned few new ones into players.

Honest limits

  • Mostly bots. Most tickets come from automated mint bots that call mint() and take the side the contract assigns, a coin flip. Only a few wallets ever chose a side with up() or down() (see api/picks.json). A record here is mostly chance and proves no skill.
  • Concentrated. A small group of wallets that come back every round makes most of the tickets (see transparency.html). Who uses it, and how much, with the builder's wallet shown separately.
  • Value 0. No native value has moved: every call is value 0 and no contract is payable. No prize, reward or token; the builder promises no value. A 1 USDT/day prize was announced on 20 November and cancelled before it started; it was never paid.
  • Unaudited. Unit and mainnet-fork tests and separate reviewer runs, but no audit firm.
  • Cannot be fixed. No owner and no proxy also means a bug cannot be patched: a fix is a new contract, and the old one stays as it is.
  • Depends on others. On the Agent ID registry and the Chainlink feeds. A round nobody settles within 7 days, or with no usable feed price, is void.
